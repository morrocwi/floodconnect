# Thai flood field evidence — people and animals

Status: evidence ledger / observed logistics patterns / not a universal inventory standard.

## Hat Yai 2025

Observed/reported mechanisms:
- dogs and cats evacuated with owners into flood response/shelter settings;
- animal illness, death and food shortage during flooding;
- mobile veterinary teams, animal medicines and dog/cat food;
- donation needs included dry/wet pet food, cat litter, towels, pee pads, leashes/collars and bowls;
- NIA relief included items for people and animals, including cat litter.

Operational extraction:
- companion animals can be co-evacuating household dependents;
- pet food/water/medicine/waste/containment are separate resource classes;
- animal-compatible shelter and transport affect whether a route is usable for the whole household unit.

Sources:
- https://dld.go.th/webnew/index.php/dld-news/head/head-moac/2569/25681201-3
- https://vet.psu.ac.th/?p=8752
- https://www.nia.or.th/index.php/Inundation-hatyai-2025

## Chiang Mai 2024

Chiang Mai Night Safari accepted animal donations including:
- dog and cat food;
- pig, goat, sheep and chicken feed;
- drinking water;
- bowls;
- cat litter;
- blankets and other necessities.

Operational extraction:
- Thai flood animal logistics extend beyond dogs/cats;
- companion animals and livestock should not be forced into one shelter pathway;
- feed type is species-specific and must remain a separate ledger.

Source:
- https://chiangmainightsafari.com/th/news/

## Nan 2026

Livestock authorities distributed hay, TMR, pet food and animal survival bags.

Operational extraction:
- large-animal/livestock support often needs separate feed, space and transport nodes;
- animal support can run in parallel with human relief rather than being a child field of food aid.

Source:
- https://region5.dld.go.th/

## Global evacuation evidence

Pet-related barriers can affect human evacuation. Studies and reviews report that lack of carriers, transport and pet-accepting shelter can contribute to evacuation failure or refusal.

Sources:
- Heath et al. (2001), DOI 10.1093/aje/153.7.659
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5551593/
- https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/emergency-preparedness/

## Evidence boundary

These cases establish the existence of recurring animal-related needs and movement constraints. They do not establish one universal quantity of pet food, litter, water or shelter space. Quantities must come from measured/declared household or veterinary inputs.
